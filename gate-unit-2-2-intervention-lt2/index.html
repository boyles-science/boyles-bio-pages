Terms
  • mutation: a change in the order of bases in DNA.
  • copying error: a wrong base added when a cell copies its DNA, which the cell does not fix.
  • mutagen: something in the environment that damages DNA and causes mutations. Examples: UV light, X-rays, some chemicals in tobacco smoke.
  • body cell: any cell that is not a sperm or egg, or a cell that makes them. Skin, lung and liver cells are body cells.
  • sex cell: a sperm or an egg. The cells in the testes and ovaries that make sperm and eggs can also pass a mutation on.

Part 1. Where do mutations come from?

The idea: Mutations have two causes. (1) Copying errors: a cell copies its DNA before it divides. Sometimes a wrong base is added and not fixed. (2) Mutagens: something in the environment damages the DNA.

Part 2. Which cell matters?

The idea: A child gets DNA from only two cells: one sperm and one egg. A mutation can reach a child only if it is in that sperm or egg. A mutation in a body cell stays in that one person.
