General senses are spread through the body, such as touch and pain. Special senses have their own organs: vision, hearing, taste, smell, and balance.

Vision

How you see

  • Light enters through the cornea and pupil. The iris changes the pupil size.
  • The lens focuses the light onto the retina.
  • Rods work in dim light. Cones detect color and detail. Most cones are at the fovea.
  • The optic nerve carries signals to the brain. The brain makes the image.

The image on the retina is upside down. The brain turns it right-side up.

Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 9 · Thu, Dec 3slide 6 of 17
Corrective lenses

Fixing focus

  • In nearsightedness, the image focuses in front of the retina. A concave lens fixes it.
  • In farsightedness, the image focuses behind the retina. A convex lens fixes it.
  • The lens changes shape to focus near and far objects.

Hearing, smell, and taste

Other special senses

  • Sound waves move the eardrum. The three ossicles pass the vibration to the cochlea, where hair cells make signals.
  • Semicircular canals in the inner ear detect head movement for balance.
  • Smell receptors in the nose connect directly to the brain's smell center.
  • Taste buds detect five tastes: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami.

Partner stations
Test your senses

Sense stations

1
Blind spot. Close your left eye. Look at the cross on the figure below with your right eye. Move closer or farther from the screen until the dot disappears.
2
Two-point test. Your partner touches the eraser ends of two pencils, 1 cm apart, to your fingertip. Say "one" or "two." Repeat on your forearm. Your eyes are closed.
3
Sound direction. Close your eyes. Your partner snaps fingers at different places around your head. Point to the sound. Then cover one ear and try again.

Touch gently with the erasers only. About 3 minutes for each check.

Summarize

How do your senses turn the world into signals?

Sensory receptors turn stimuli into nerve signals. The eye focuses light on the retina, where photoreceptors make signals. The ear turns sound into vibrations, and hair cells in the cochlea make signals. Smell and taste use chemoreceptors. Skin has several kinds of receptors.

Take-home message and exit ticket

Before you leave

Take-home message

Every sense works the same way: a receptor detects a stimulus and starts a signal. The brain makes the experience.

Write in your notebook

Pick one sense. Name the receptor type, the stimulus, and the organ that detects it. Then explain what the brain does with the signal.
