Receptors

Types of sensory receptors

Receptor typeDetects
MechanoreceptorPressure, touch, stretch, sound
ThermoreceptorTemperature change
NociceptorTissue damage (pain)
PhotoreceptorLight
ChemoreceptorChemicals in the nose, tongue, and blood

General senses are spread through the body, such as touch and pain. Special senses have their own organs: vision, hearing, taste, smell, and balance.

General example. Each receptor type is like a different sensor on a car: one for speed, one for temperature, one for light.

Specific example. Pain receptors in your skin signal tissue damage.

Vision

How you see

  • Light enters through the cornea and pupil. The iris changes the pupil size.
  • The lens focuses the light onto the retina.
  • Rods work in dim light. Cones detect color and detail. Most cones are at the fovea.
  • The optic nerve carries signals to the brain. The brain makes the image.

The image on the retina is upside down. The brain turns it right-side up.

General example. The eye works like a camera. The lens focuses light onto the retina, which is like the camera's sensor.

Specific example. In dim light, rods help you see shapes at night.

Summarize

How do your senses turn the world into signals?

Sensory receptors turn stimuli into nerve signals. The eye focuses light on the retina, where photoreceptors make signals. The ear turns sound into vibrations, and hair cells in the cochlea make signals. Smell and taste use chemoreceptors. Skin has several kinds of receptors.
