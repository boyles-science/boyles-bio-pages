Essential question

How does a neuron turn a change in charge into a signal that travels?

A neuron signal is an action potential. It is a quick change in the electrical charge across the axon's membrane.

Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 3 · Wed, Nov 18slide 3 of 27
Notebook · Notes box 1
Resting neuron

The neuron at rest

  • The inside of a resting neuron is negative compared with the outside. It is about -70 mV.
  • mV means millivolt, one thousandth of a volt.
  • The sodium-potassium pump moves 3 sodium ions (Na⁺) out and 2 potassium ions (K⁺) in.
  • Some K⁺ also leaks out. This keeps the inside negative.

General example. A resting neuron is like a charged battery that is waiting to be used.

Specific example. A neuron at -70 mV inside is ready to fire when a strong enough stimulus arrives.

Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 3 · Wed, Nov 18slide 4 of 27
Notebook · Notes box 2
Threshold and depolarization

The neuron fires

  • A stimulus opens a few Na⁺ channels. The inside becomes a little less negative.
  • At the threshold, about -55 mV, many Na⁺ channels open.
  • Na⁺ rushes in. The inside becomes positive, up to about +30 mV.
  • This is depolarization.

General example. Reaching threshold is like a domino tipping. Once it tips, it falls all the way.

Specific example. A light touch may not reach threshold. A firm press does, so the neuron fires.

Reading the graph

One action potential

  • 1 Resting, about -70 mV.
  • 2 Depolarization: Na⁺ enters.
  • 3 Repolarization: K⁺ leaves.
  • 4 Hyperpolarization: a dip below -70 mV.
  • 5 Back to resting.

Summarize

How does a neuron turn a change in charge into a signal that travels?

A neuron at rest is about -70 mV inside. A stimulus that reaches threshold, about -55 mV, opens Na⁺ channels, and Na⁺ rushes in. The inside becomes positive. Then the membrane returns toward rest.

Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 4 · Thu, Nov 19slide 16 of 27
Notebook · Notes box 1
Channels

Two kinds of gated channels

  • Sodium channels open first. Na⁺ goes in.
  • They then become inactive.
  • Potassium channels open a little later. K⁺ goes out.
  • The pump and leak channels then restore -70 mV.

General example. Channels are like gates that open for one kind of ion.

Specific example. A numbing medicine at the dentist blocks sodium channels, so no action potential forms and you feel no pain.

Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 4 · Thu, Nov 19slide 17 of 27
Notebook · Notes box 2
Traveling down the axon

Myelin speeds the signal

  • In an axon with myelin, ions can cross the membrane only at the nodes.
  • The action potential jumps from node to node. This is called saltatory conduction.
  • This is much faster than a signal that moves along every part of the membrane.
  • A thicker axon also carries signals faster.

General example. A driver on an open highway moves faster than one stopping at every light. Myelin is like the highway with few stops.

Specific example. In multiple sclerosis, damaged myelin slows signals. A person may feel numbness or weakness.

Partner stations
Signal relay

Model a nerve signal

1
Round 1. Make a circle of six students. Each person gently squeezes the next person's hand when they feel a squeeze. Time one trip around the circle with the Chromebook stopwatch. You may tap the desk instead.
2
Round 2: myelin model. Now only every second person is a node. Each node taps the next node, two people away. The people in between stay still. Time it.
3
Compare. Divide each time by the number of people who passed the signal. Which model was faster?

Do each round twice and use the faster time. You may time and record for your group instead.

Summarize

How does a neuron turn a change in charge into a signal that travels?

Sodium channels open first, then potassium channels. An action potential is all or none. A neuron has a short refractory period after it fires. Myelin makes the signal jump from node to node, so it travels faster.
