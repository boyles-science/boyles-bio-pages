Essential question

How is a neuron built to carry a signal?

Neurons are the cells that carry signals. Neuroglia are the cells that support and protect them.

Notebook · Notes box 1
Neuron structure

The parts of a neuron

  • Dendrites receive signals from other cells.
  • The cell body holds the nucleus.
  • The axon carries the signal away from the cell body.
  • Axon terminals release neurotransmitters, chemicals that pass the signal to the next cell.

Nodes of Ranvier are shown for interest. They are nice to know, but you do not need to memorize them.

General example. A neuron is like a one-way street. Signals come in at the dendrites and leave at the axon terminals.

Specific example. In a sensory neuron from your fingertip, the signal starts at the touch ending and travels toward your spinal cord.

Insulation

Myelin: the fast lane

  • Myelin is a fatty layer that wraps around many axons.
  • Myelin works like the plastic around a wire. It keeps the signal inside the axon.
  • Nodes of Ranvier are the small gaps between myelin sections. This is nice to know. You do not need to remember the name.
  • Myelinated axons carry signals much faster than bare axons. You will see how in the next lessons.

General example. Myelin is like the plastic coating around an electrical wire.

Specific example. In multiple sclerosis, the immune system damages myelin, and signals slow down.

Three kinds of neurons

Sensory, inter, and motor

  • Sensory neurons carry signals from receptors toward the brain and spinal cord.
  • Interneurons connect neurons inside the central nervous system.
  • Motor neurons carry commands away to muscles and glands.

General example. Sensory neurons are the lanes coming in. Motor neurons are the lanes going out. Interneurons are the roads that connect them.

Specific example. You touch a hot pan. A sensory neuron carries the signal in, an interneuron links it in the spinal cord, and a motor neuron tells your arm to pull away.

Neuroglia

The support cells

  • Neuroglia (also called glial cells) do not carry signals.
  • They support, feed, and protect neurons.
  • There are more than a few types. Four are shown here.

General example. Neuroglia are like the support crew behind a concert. They do not perform, but the show needs them.

Specific example. After a head injury, microglia gather to clear away damaged cells.

Notebook · Notes box 3
More neuroglia

Two more to know

  • Ependymal cells line the fluid-filled spaces in the brain. They help make cerebrospinal fluid, which cushions the brain.
  • Satellite cells surround neuron cell bodies in the peripheral nervous system.
  • A ganglion is a cluster of neuron cell bodies outside the central nervous system.

The central nervous system has astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, and ependymal cells. The peripheral nervous system has Schwann cells and satellite cells.

General example. Ependymal cells are like the pipes that line a water system.

Specific example. Ependymal cells in the brain's fluid spaces help make cerebrospinal fluid, which cushions your brain.

Notebook · Notes box 3
A fact worth knowing

One cell can be over a meter long

A single motor neuron can reach from the spinal cord to the foot. Its axon can be over one meter long.

Most mature neurons cannot divide. Because of this, damage to nerve tissue can be hard to repair.

General example. Some neurons are like a long cable that runs down a whole leg.

Specific example. A motor neuron from your lower spine to your big toe can be about a meter long.

Partner stations
Draw it, match it, sort it

Neuron stations

1
Draw it. Close your slides. Draw a neuron in your notebook. Label the dendrites, cell body, nucleus, axon, myelin, and axon terminals. Then check your drawing against the diagram.
2
Match it. Your partner reads the job of a part. You name the part. Use only the parts you labeled. Swap after five parts.
3
Sort it. Your partner reads a description. You say which of these it is: sensory neuron, motor neuron, interneuron, astrocyte, oligodendrocyte, microglia, or Schwann cell. Do at least six.

Stay in your seat. About 3 minutes for each check.

Summarize

How is a neuron built to carry a signal?

Neurons carry signals. A neuron has dendrites that receive signals, a cell body, and an axon that carries the signal away. Myelin speeds the signal. Sensory, inter, and motor neurons do different jobs. Neuroglia support and protect neurons.
