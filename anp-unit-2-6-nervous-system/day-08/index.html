The spinal cord

Where it is

  • The spinal cord runs from the brain down the vertebral canal, inside the vertebrae.
  • It ends near the first or second lumbar vertebra.
  • Meninges and cerebrospinal fluid surround it.
  • 31 pairs of spinal nerves leave it: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, and 1 coccygeal.

Notebook · Notes box 2
Two directions

Signals up and down

  • Ascending tracts carry sensory signals up to the brain.
  • Descending tracts carry motor commands down from the brain.
  • The dorsal root brings sensory signals in. The ventral root sends motor signals out.
  • Gray matter forms the butterfly shape. White matter surrounds it.

Reflexes

Fast and automatic

  • A reflex is a fast, automatic response.
  • In a stretch reflex, such as the knee-jerk, a muscle that is stretched contracts.
  • In a withdrawal reflex, you pull away from something painful.
  • The brain is told about the reflex after it begins.

Notebook · Notes box 3
Spinal cord injury

Level matters

  • A spinal cord injury can block signals going up and down.
  • The higher the injury, the more of the body can be affected.
  • Signals below the injury cannot pass.

Neurons in the spinal cord do not repair easily, so many injuries are lasting.

Notebook · Back page: Stations
Partner stations
Test a reflex

Reflex stations

1
Knee-jerk. Sit on a chair so your legs hang free. Your partner gently taps just below the kneecap with the side of the hand. Watch your leg. Switch roles.
2
Draw it. Without looking at the slide, draw the five parts of a reflex arc. Add arrows for the signal.
3
Put it in order. Put these in order: motor neuron fires, receptor detects, effector responds, interneuron processes, sensory neuron carries the signal.

Tap gently. Stop if anything hurts. You may watch and record instead. About 3 minutes for each check.

Summarize

Why can your body respond before you have time to think?

The spinal cord carries signals between the brain and the body, and it can also act on its own. A reflex arc has five parts: receptor, sensory neuron, integration center, motor neuron, and effector. A reflex is faster than a choice because the signal does not have to reach the brain first.
