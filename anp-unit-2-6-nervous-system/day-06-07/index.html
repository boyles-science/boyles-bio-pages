The cerebrum

The largest part

  • The cerebrum has a left and a right half, called hemispheres.
  • The corpus callosum is a band of axons that connects them.
  • The outer layer is the cerebral cortex. Its folds give it a large surface area.
  • The cortex handles thinking, senses, and voluntary movement.

Summarize

How does each part of the brain contribute to what you do?

The cerebrum has two hemispheres joined by the corpus callosum. Its outer layer is the cerebral cortex. Each hemisphere has four lobes: frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital. Each lobe has its own jobs.

Deeper parts

Below the cerebrum

  • The thalamus relays sensory signals to the cortex.
  • The hypothalamus controls body temperature, hunger, and thirst. It also controls the pituitary gland.
  • The cerebellum coordinates balance and smooth movement.
  • The brain stem (midbrain, pons, medulla) controls breathing, heart rate, and blood pressure.
Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 7 · Tue, Dec 1slide 17 of 27
Protection

Keeping the brain safe

  • The skull surrounds the brain.
  • Meninges are three membranes: dura mater, arachnoid mater, and pia mater.
  • Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) cushions the brain and spinal cord.
  • The blood-brain barrier controls which substances reach the brain.
Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 7 · Tue, Dec 1slide 18 of 27
Gray and white matter

Two kinds of tissue

  • Gray matter is mostly cell bodies and dendrites. It forms the cortex.
  • White matter is mostly myelinated axons. It forms the pathways inside.
  • The brain uses about one fifth of your body's oxygen. It is only a small part of your body weight.

Summarize

How does each part of the brain contribute to what you do?

The thalamus relays sensory signals. The hypothalamus controls temperature, hunger, and thirst. The cerebellum coordinates balance. The brain stem controls breathing and heart rate. The skull, meninges, cerebrospinal fluid, and blood-brain barrier protect the brain.
