Essential question

How does your body sense, decide, and respond so fast?

The nervous system is the body's fast communication network. It uses electrical signals to carry information between your senses, your brain, and your muscles.

Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 1 · Mon, Nov 16slide 3 of 17
Three jobs

Sense, decide, respond

  • Sensory input: receptors detect a change, such as heat, light, or pressure. Nerves carry the information toward the brain and spinal cord.
  • Integration: the brain and spinal cord process the information and decide what to do.
  • Motor output: nerves carry a command to a muscle or a gland, and it responds.
Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 1 · Mon, Nov 16slide 4 of 17
Two main divisions

Central and peripheral

  • Central nervous system (CNS): the brain and the spinal cord. It is the control center. It does the integration.
  • Peripheral nervous system (PNS): all the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord. It carries signals into the CNS and out of it.

Central means in the middle. Peripheral means around the edge.

Inside the PNS

Signals in, signals out

  • Sensory division: carries signals from receptors into the CNS.
  • Motor division: carries commands from the CNS out to muscles and glands.

Sensory nerves bring news in. Motor nerves carry orders out.

Inside the motor division

Who is in control?

  • Somatic nervous system: controls your skeletal muscles. You can choose these movements.
  • Autonomic nervous system: controls smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, and glands. It works automatically.

You may remember voluntary and involuntary muscle from the Muscular System unit.

Unit 2.6 Nervous System · Day 1 · Mon, Nov 16slide 7 of 17
Inside the autonomic system

Two opposite teams

  • Sympathetic: prepares the body for action. The heart beats faster. The pupils get larger. Digestion slows down.
  • Parasympathetic: returns the body to rest. The heart slows. The pupils get smaller. Digestion speeds up.

The two teams balance each other. This helps keep the body in homeostasis.

A number worth knowing

Fast signals travel about 100 meters per second

The fastest nerve fibers carry signals at about 100 meters per second. At that speed, a signal could travel the length of your body in about two hundredths of a second.

Signals in other nerve fibers travel much more slowly. Today you will measure how fast your own body responds.

Partner stations
Three quick checks

Your nervous system at work

1
Ruler drop. Two 15 cm rulers are taped end to end to make one 30 cm ruler. Your partner holds the top end, with the 0 cm end down. Hold your thumb and finger open around the 0 cm end. Your partner lets go without warning. Catch it. Read where your thumb is, in centimeters. If it is on the second ruler, add 15. Do 3 tries.
2
Pupils. Cover your eyes with your hands for 15 seconds. Uncover them. Your partner watches your pupils, the dark circles in your eyes, and says what changes.
3
Blinking. Try not to blink for 20 seconds. Your partner counts how many times you blink anyway. Notice how it feels.

Stay in your place. Stop if your eyes feel uncomfortable. About 2 minutes for each check.

Summarize

How does your body sense, decide, and respond so fast?

The nervous system senses, decides, and responds. Sensory nerves carry signals into the central nervous system. The brain and spinal cord decide. Motor nerves carry the command out. The somatic system moves skeletal muscle. The autonomic system runs organs and glands automatically.
