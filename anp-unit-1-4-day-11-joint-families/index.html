Joint families

No supplies needed. You need your notebook, a pencil, and this page.

Step 1 · Meet the three joint families

A joint is a place where two bones meet. Joints are sorted into three families by how they are built.

Do this in your notebook. Finish the sentences in today's Write This column. Use the cards below. Then answer the warm up: Which joint in your body moves the most?

Fibrous joints

Movement
Stability

Little or no movement. Example: the sutures (seams) between skull bones. Built for protection.

Cartilaginous joints

Movement
Stability

Limited movement. Example: the discs between the vertebrae (back bones). They absorb force.

Synovial joints

Movement
Stability

The most movement. They have a joint cavity (a space inside the joint). Examples: shoulder, elbow, knee.

Step 3 · The trade-off

A joint that moves a lot is easier to injure. A joint that is safe cannot move much. Every joint is a trade.

Do this. Move the slider from stable to free movement. Read what each family gives up.
