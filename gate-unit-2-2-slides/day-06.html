Parts of a scientific argument

Listen first, then copy into your notebook.

Write this:
  • Claim: your answer to the question.
  • Evidence: data that supports the claim.
  • Reasoning: the science idea that links the evidence to the claim.
  • Counterclaim: another explanation. Rebuttal: evidence showing it is weaker.

Deciding the source

Listen first, then copy into your notebook.

Write this:
  • If the allele is in the family, a new combination is likely.
  • If it is new and there was a mutagen, an environmental mutation is likely.
  • If it is new with no mutagen, a copying error is likely.

Terms

Arguing from evidence

  • Claim: your answer.
  • Evidence: data that supports it.
  • Reasoning: the science idea linking them.
  • Counterclaim: another possible explanation.
  • Rebuttal: evidence showing the counterclaim is weaker.

Red hair

Two parents have dark brown hair. Their child has red hair. A grandparent on each side had red hair. Red hair is linked to versions of the MC1R gene; a child usually needs two red-hair alleles.
