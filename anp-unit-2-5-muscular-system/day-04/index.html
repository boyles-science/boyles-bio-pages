Focused notes

From small to large

  • Myofilaments are tiny protein strands: actin (thin) and myosin (thick).
  • Myofilaments line up in repeating units called sarcomeres. A chain of sarcomeres is a myofibril.
  • Many myofibrils fill one muscle fiber. Fibers group into fascicles, and fascicles make the whole muscle.

Myofilaments

Actin and myosin proteins that slide to cause contraction. The repeating units they form are called sarcomeres.

Level 2

Myofibrils

Long chains of sarcomeres inside a muscle fiber. They run parallel to each other through the length of the fiber.

Muscle fiber (cell)

An elongated, multinucleated cell that is surrounded by the sarcolemma and contains many myofibrils.

A helpful comparison

Muscle is like a cable

Small wires (myofilaments) form strands (myofibrils), which bundle into cords (fibers and fascicles), which wrap together to form the main cable (whole muscle).

Focused notes

The wrappings

  • Each level is wrapped in connective tissue, like plastic wrap around a bundle.
  • Endomysium wraps each fiber. Perimysium wraps each fascicle. Epimysium wraps the whole muscle.
  • Memory trick: endo = inside, peri = around, epi = on top.

Focused notes

Wrappings become tendons

  • At the ends of a muscle, all the wrappings join together to form a tendon.
  • The tendon attaches the muscle to the bone.
  • When the muscle shortens, the tendon pulls the bone and you move.

Lab · 20 minutes · groups of 3–4 · Part 1 of 2

Muscle-in-Layers Model: Build it

Build a model muscle from the smallest level up. Paper strips are the muscle parts. Yarn is the connective tissue wrapping. Build first (about 10 min). Don't wrap anything until a step tells you to.

  1. Myofilaments. Count out 24 paper strips. Each strip is one myofilament. Write A (actin, thin) on 12 strips and M (myosin, thick) on the other 12.
  2. Myofibrils. Stack 3 strips, mixing A and M. Tape them together around the middle. Make 8.
  3. Muscle fibers + endomysium. Hold 2 myofibrils side by side. Wrap a short yarn piece around them 3 times and tie it. Make 4. This yarn is the endomysium.
  4. Fascicles + perimysium. Hold 2 fibers together. Wrap them with a medium yarn piece and tie it. Make 2. This yarn is the perimysium.
  5. Whole muscle + epimysium. Hold both fascicles together. Wrap the long yarn piece around the outside and tie it. This yarn is the epimysium.
Materials (per group)
24 paper strips (about 1 in × 8 in)
Yarn, about 3 yd total, cut into:
 • 4 short (12 in) = endomysium
 • 2 medium (18 in) = perimysium
 • 1 long (24 in) = epimysium
Tape, 1 marker, 6 paper tags
Check your count
24 myofilaments → 8 myofibrils → 4 fibers → 2 fascicles → 1 muscle
7 yarn wraps in total

Tag it, explain it, record it

  1. Make a tendon. At one end of your muscle, twist all the yarn ends together. This is where the wrappings join to form a tendon.
  2. Tag every level. Write a paper tag for each level and tape it on one example: myofilament, myofibril, muscle fiber, fascicle, whole muscle, tendon. On the fiber, fascicle, and muscle tags, also write the wrapping name.
  3. Explain it (about 5 min). Pair up with another group. Each person explains one level using the frame below. Then listen while they explain theirs.
