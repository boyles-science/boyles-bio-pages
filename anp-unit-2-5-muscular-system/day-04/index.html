Objective

Today's goal

Students will be able to describe the structural organization of muscle from smallest to largest level, by building it up one level at a time, so that they can explain how small molecular interactions lead to coordinated movement.

  • I can describe the structural organization of muscle from smallest to largest level.
  • I can identify and label connective tissue layers: endomysium, perimysium, and epimysium.
  • I can differentiate between a muscle fiber, fascicle, and whole muscle.

Level 1

Myofilaments

Actin and myosin proteins that slide to cause contraction. The repeating units they form are called sarcomeres.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 4 · Thu, Oct 22slide 3 of 14
Level 2

Myofibrils

Long chains of sarcomeres inside a muscle fiber. They run parallel to each other through the length of the fiber.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 4 · Thu, Oct 22slide 4 of 14
Level 3

Muscle fiber (cell)

An elongated, multinucleated cell that is surrounded by the sarcolemma and contains many myofibrils.

Level 4 and 5

Fascicle and whole muscle

Fascicle

A bundle of muscle fibers.

Whole muscle

A group of fascicles, attached to bone by tendons.

myofilament → myofibril → muscle fiber → fascicle → whole muscle

A helpful comparison

Muscle is like a cable

Small wires (myofilaments) form strands (myofibrils), which bundle into cords (fibers and fascicles), which wrap together to form the main cable (whole muscle).
