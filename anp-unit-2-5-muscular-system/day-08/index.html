System 1

Creatine phosphate

A quick energy source. It regenerates ATP from phosphate and ADP during short bursts of intense activity.

Provides 1 ATP per creatine phosphate. Occurs for the first ~10 seconds of exercise, before other systems kick in.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 8 · Wed, Oct 28slide 4 of 15
System 2

Glycolysis

Breaks down glucose into pyruvate, which leads to lactic acid, for a rapid supply of ATP without oxygen.

Provides 2 ATP per glucose. The primary pathway during high-intensity exercise lasting about 30–60 seconds.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 8 · Wed, Oct 28slide 5 of 15
System 3

Aerobic respiration

Occurs in the presence of oxygen, generating the most ATP for prolonged, lower-intensity activities. Uses fat and carbohydrates as fuel (and can also use amino acids).

Yields 36–38 ATP per glucose. Can supply energy long-term, during endurance exercise.

What happens when oxygen runs out?

Lactic acid and fatigue

Lactic acid accumulates, and fatigue sets in.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 8 · Wed, Oct 28slide 7 of 15
What happens afterward?

Oxygen debt

"Oxygen debt" occurs as the body restores ATP and removes lactic acid after exercise ends.

Tie to feedback mechanisms

This is homeostasis and recovery: the body senses the imbalance from exercise and works to restore its resting state.

Do this
Stress ball squeeze test

Collect your data

1
Pair up or work in groups of 2–4.
2
Each student squeezes a stress ball or dynamometer repeatedly for 1 minute while a partner counts repetitions, recording the count every 10 seconds.
3
Rest for 1 minute, then repeat 2–3 times. Graph "time vs. repetitions" for each trial.
