Recap · Day 6

Contraction types, so far

There are isometric and isotonic contractions. Isotonic breaks down further into concentric and eccentric. Regardless of the type, cross-bridges always form between actin and myosin — they just do different jobs: holding length (isometric), sliding actin inward (concentric), or controlling lengthening (eccentric).

Anatomy of the NMJ

Three key parts

  • The motor neuron axon terminal — the end of the nerve cell.
  • The synaptic cleft — the gap between the neuron and the muscle.
  • The motor end-plate — the muscle fiber's membrane at the junction.

The messenger

Acetylcholine (ACh)

The neurotransmitter is acetylcholine (ACh). It is released, diffuses across the gap, binds to receptors, and opens ion channels on the muscle fiber.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 7 · Tue, Oct 27slide 6 of 17
Motor activation

Four steps

1
Arrival. An action potential reaches the synapse.
2
Release. ACh diffuses across the gap.
3
Binding. Receptors on the muscle fiber are activated, and ion channels open.
4
Depolarization. The muscle fiber initiates contraction.

What happens next

Once the muscle fiber is triggered, calcium is released, leading to the actin-myosin interaction that causes contraction.

Looking ahead

One motor neuron, many fibers

A motor unit is one motor neuron plus all the muscle fibers it controls. Motor units vary in size: small units allow fine control, and larger units allow greater force.

You'll go deeper on motor units and force control on Day 9.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 7 · Tue, Oct 27slide 9 of 17
Force control, in brief

Recruitment and the size principle

  • Recruitment: recruiting more motor units causes more fibers to contract.
  • Summation: more fibers contracting leads to greater force.
  • Principle: smaller units activate first; only small units activate when fine control is needed.
  • Activation: different fibers stimulate asynchronously.

What happens if a step is blocked?

No ACh, no contraction

Without ACh, the receptors on the motor end-plate are never activated, so depolarization never happens and no cross-bridges form.

The muscle is weak or paralyzed — even though the fiber itself, the ATP supply, and the filaments are all normal.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 7 · Tue, Oct 27slide 13 of 17
Why this matters

One gap, one messenger, many steps

Every skeletal muscle contraction you make — blinking, walking, writing — starts at this same tiny gap.

A problem anywhere along this path can stop or weaken a contraction, even if every other part of the muscle works perfectly.
