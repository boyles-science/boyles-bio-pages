Focused notes

Parts of a lever

  • Every lever has a fulcrum (the pivot), an effort (the push or pull), and a load (what gets moved).
  • In your body, the joint is the fulcrum, the muscle is the effort, and the body part or weight is the load.
  • Tendons carry the muscle's pull to the bone.

Notebook p. 81 · Focused Notes: Parts of a lever
The three parts

Every lever has three parts

  • The pivot (fulcrum) — the fixed point the lever turns around. In the body, a joint.
  • The effort — the force that moves the lever. In the body, a muscle pulling on a bone.
  • The load — the weight or resistance being moved. In the body, the body part plus whatever it's carrying.

Focused notes

Three classes of levers

  • 1st class: the fulcrum is in the middle, like nodding your head.
  • 2nd class: the load is in the middle, like rising onto your tiptoes.
  • 3rd class: the effort is in the middle, like a biceps curl. This is the most common lever in the body.

Notebook p. 81 · Focused Notes: Three classes of levers
Class 1

Pivot in the middle

The pivot sits between the effort and the load — like a seesaw.

Body example: tipping your head back. The joint at the top of the spine is the pivot, neck muscles pull at the back (effort), and the front of the skull is the load.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 14 · Thu, Nov 5slide 9 of 35
Notebook p. 81 · Focused Notes: Three classes of levers
Class 2

Load in the middle

The load sits between the pivot and the effort — like a wheelbarrow.

Body example: rising onto your tiptoes. The ball of the foot is the pivot, your body weight is the load in the middle, and the calf muscles pulling up on the heel are the effort.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 14 · Thu, Nov 5slide 10 of 35
Notebook p. 81 · Focused Notes: Three classes of levers
Class 3

Effort in the middle

The effort sits between the pivot and the load — the most common lever type in the body.

Body example: a biceps curl. The elbow is the pivot, the biceps brachii pulls in the middle of the forearm (effort), and the hand plus whatever it holds is the load.

Focused notes

Why the body uses 3rd class levers

  • Third-class levers need a lot of muscle force.
  • In exchange, they move the hand or foot fast and far.
  • That trade gives us speed and range of motion for throwing, running, and kicking.

Notebook p. 82 · Focused Notes: Why the body uses 3rd class levers
Connecting the systems

Muscle and bone, working together

A lever cannot move without a force. The muscular system supplies that force at each joint.

This is why the muscular and skeletal systems are studied together: bones give the levers their shape, and muscles supply the effort.
