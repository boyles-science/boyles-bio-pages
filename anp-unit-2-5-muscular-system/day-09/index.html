Focused notes

One twitch

  • A muscle twitch is one contraction from one signal.
  • It has three parts: the latent period (a short delay), the contraction phase (tension rises), and the relaxation phase (tension falls).
  • During the latent period, calcium is being released but tension has not risen yet.

One stimulus, one twitch

The muscle twitch

A muscle twitch is a brief, involuntary contraction of a muscle fiber (or group of fibers), following a single stimulus such as a nerve impulse.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 9 · Thu, Oct 29slide 7 of 34
Notebook p. 53 · Focused Notes: One twitch
Three phases

Latent, contraction, relaxation

1
Latent period. Between the stimulus and the start of contraction — just a few milliseconds.
2
Contraction phase. Muscle fibers shorten, generating force. Its length can vary with fiber type and stimulus strength.
3
Relaxation phase. The fibers return to their resting state — this usually takes longer than the contraction itself.

Focused notes

Signals that add up

  • If a second signal arrives before the fiber relaxes, the twitches add up and get stronger. That is summation.
  • Very fast signals make the twitches blend into one smooth, strong pull. That is tetanus.
  • Almost every smooth movement you make is tetanus. (This is not the disease called tetanus.)
Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 9 · Thu, Oct 29slide 10 of 34
Notebook p. 53 · Focused Notes: Signals that add up
Faster signals

Summation and tetanus

Summation: repeated stimuli arriving before the fiber relaxes combine for a stronger contraction.

Tetanus: if signals come fast enough, the fiber never relaxes at all, producing one steady, strong pull.

Focused notes

Two ways to get stronger

  • Frequency: send signals faster, so twitches add up.
  • Recruitment: turn on more motor units.
  • When some motor units get tired and drop out, the pull becomes uneven and your muscle shakes.

The functional unit

Motor units

A motor unit is a single motor neuron and all the muscle fibers it controls.

When the motor neuron fires, it triggers an action potential that stimulates every fiber in its unit at once, causing them to contract together.

Notebook p. 54 · Focused Notes: Two ways to get stronger
The size principle

Small units fire first

Motor unit recruitment follows the size principle: smaller motor units are activated first, followed by larger units as more force is needed.

This gradation lets you make smooth, controlled movements instead of all-or-nothing jerks.

Notebook p. 54 · Focused Notes: Two ways to get stronger
What decides contraction strength?

Four factors

1
Recruitment. More motor units firing means a stronger contraction.
2
Size. Larger motor units (more fibers) generate more force.
3
Frequency. Faster stimulation boosts strength through summation and tetanus.
4
Length. A muscle fiber at its optimal length produces the most force.

Notebook p. 54 · Focused Notes: Two ways to get stronger
Why it matters

Shaking and smoothness

Shaking happens when motor units alternate between firing and fatiguing.

Smooth movement comes from precise recruitment and tetanus, achieved through controlled nerve signaling — this is how the nervous system controls movement and helps maintain posture.
