Essential question

What does the muscular system actually do for you?

The muscular system is crucial for movement and maintaining posture. It also helps you breathe, regulate body temperature, and perform daily activities efficiently.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 1 · Mon, Oct 19slide 3 of 12
Function 1 and 2

Movement and posture

  • Movement: muscle contraction involves the shortening of muscle fibers, allowing movement and support for the body. Nerve signals transmit electrical impulses that control muscle contraction and relaxation, for precise movements.
  • Posture: muscles hold your joints steady and keep you stable while sitting or standing.

Function 3

Heat production

Muscle contractions release heat as a byproduct of ATP use, which helps maintain body temperature. Shivering muscles generate extra heat to keep the body warm in cold conditions.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 1 · Mon, Oct 19slide 5 of 12
Function 4

Circulation

Cardiac muscle contractions pump blood throughout the body, delivering oxygen and nutrients. Smooth muscles in blood vessel walls regulate circulation by constricting or relaxing to control blood flow.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 1 · Mon, Oct 19slide 6 of 12
Function 5

Moving substances through the body

Smooth muscles in the digestive tract contract rhythmically — called peristalsis — to move food along. These involuntary contractions break down and propel food so nutrients can be absorbed.

Voluntary vs. involuntary

Who's in control?

Muscles are classified as voluntary, which we control, or involuntary, which function automatically.

  • Skeletal muscles are under our control and move the body by contracting and relaxing.
  • Smooth and cardiac muscles function automatically to regulate processes like digestion, circulation, and heartbeat.

A number worth knowing

Muscle is about 40% of your body weight

Regular exercise significantly improves muscle strength and endurance. It also helps prevent muscle atrophy and age-related decline.

Proper nutrition — including protein — is vital for muscle repair and growth.
