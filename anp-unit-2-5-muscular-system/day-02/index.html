Objective

Today's goal

Students will be able to tell the three types of muscle tissue apart, by comparing their shape, control, and location, so that they can identify a tissue sample or symptom as skeletal, cardiac, or smooth muscle.

  • I can name the three types of muscle tissue.
  • I can describe each type's shape and whether it is voluntary or involuntary.
  • I can identify where each type is found in the body.
Language target

I can compare the three muscle types. I give a reason for each difference.

Key words: skeletal muscle, smooth muscle, cardiac muscle

Sentence frame: Smooth muscle ____, but skeletal muscle ____. This explains why ____.

Type 1

Skeletal muscle

  • Voluntary — you decide when it contracts.
  • Striated (striped) under a microscope.
  • Each cell (fiber) has many nuclei — a fiber forms when many smaller cells fuse together during development.
  • Attaches to bone by tendons and moves the skeleton.

Type 2

Cardiac muscle

  • Involuntary — you do not control it.
  • Striated, but cells branch and connect at dark lines (intercalated discs).
  • Each cell usually has one nucleus, occasionally two.
  • Found only in the wall of the heart.

Type 3

Smooth muscle

  • Involuntary — you do not control it.
  • No stripes under a microscope.
  • Each cell is spindle-shaped, with one nucleus.
  • Rarely tires. Found in the walls of organs and blood vessels (stomach, intestines, bladder).

Why it matters

Structure fits the job

  • Skeletal muscle needs to contract fast and on command — many nuclei support fast protein-making for quick, powerful contractions.
  • Cardiac muscle needs to contract in a steady, connected rhythm — the branching and intercalated discs let the signal spread cell to cell.
  • Smooth muscle needs to hold a slow squeeze for a long time without tiring — found wherever the body needs slow, steady pressure.

Summarize

How can you tell the three types of muscle tissue apart?

You can tell muscle types apart by their stripes, their nuclei, their shape, and whether you control them. Skeletal muscle is voluntary, striped, and has many nuclei in each cell. Cardiac muscle is involuntary, striped, and branched, with one nucleus. Smooth muscle is involuntary and has no stripes.
