Focused notes

Slow-twitch fibers (Type I)

  • Slow-twitch fibers contract slowly but can keep going for a long time.
  • They are red because they have lots of myoglobin, which stores oxygen.
  • They use aerobic respiration and have many mitochondria. Great for posture and endurance.

Type 1

Slow-twitch fibers

  • Many mitochondria and lots of myoglobin (a red protein that stores oxygen) — dark red color.
  • Make ATP mainly by aerobic respiration.
  • Contract slowly, with low force, but resist fatigue for a long time.

Notebook p. 59 · Focused Notes: Fast-twitch fibers (Type II)
Focused notes

Fast-twitch fibers (Type II)

  • Fast-twitch fibers contract quickly and powerfully.
  • They are pale because they have less myoglobin.
  • They use anaerobic energy, so they tire quickly. Great for sprinting, jumping, and lifting.
Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 10 · Fri, Oct 30slide 8 of 32
Notebook p. 59 · Focused Notes: Fast-twitch fibers (Type II)
Type 2

Fast-twitch fibers

  • Few mitochondria, little myoglobin — pale color.
  • Make ATP mainly by glycolysis and creatine phosphate — anaerobic sources.
  • Contract quickly, with high force, but fatigue quickly.

Focused notes

Everyone has both

  • Most muscles are a mix of both fiber types.
  • Your genes set the starting mix. Training can make fibers stronger or more aerobic.
  • Back muscles that hold your posture all day are mostly slow-twitch.

Notebook p. 60 · Focused Notes: Everyone has both
Why it matters

Job decides fiber type

Deep back muscles that hold you upright all day work best with slow-twitch fibers — they need endurance, not power.

A muscle used for a single powerful jump benefits from fast-twitch fibers — power over endurance.

Most human muscles are a mix of both types.

Back to the warm-up

Why is chicken breast pale?

Chicken breast muscle is used for short bursts of powerful flight (or, in farmed chickens, is simply built that way) — mostly fast-twitch, pale fibers.

Chicken thigh muscle works steadily all day (standing, walking) — mostly slow-twitch, dark, myoglobin-rich fibers.
