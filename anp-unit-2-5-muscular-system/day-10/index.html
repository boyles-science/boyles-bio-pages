Type 1

Slow-twitch fibers

  • Many mitochondria and lots of myoglobin (a red protein that stores oxygen) — dark red color.
  • Make ATP mainly by aerobic respiration.
  • Contract slowly, with low force, but resist fatigue for a long time.
Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 10 · Fri, Oct 30slide 3 of 13
Type 2

Fast-twitch fibers

  • Few mitochondria, little myoglobin — pale color.
  • Make ATP mainly by glycolysis and creatine phosphate — anaerobic sources.
  • Contract quickly, with high force, but fatigue quickly.

Back to the warm-up

Why is chicken breast pale?

Chicken breast muscle is used for short bursts of powerful flight (or, in farmed chickens, is simply built that way) — mostly fast-twitch, pale fibers.

Chicken thigh muscle works steadily all day (standing, walking) — mostly slow-twitch, dark, myoglobin-rich fibers.

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 10 · Fri, Oct 30slide 8 of 13
Why it matters

Job decides fiber type

Deep back muscles that hold you upright all day work best with slow-twitch fibers — they need endurance, not power.

A muscle used for a single powerful jump benefits from fast-twitch fibers — power over endurance.

Most human muscles are a mix of both types.
