Classifying disorders

Three broad causes

Muscle disorders impair muscle function, leading to weakness, fatigue, or pain. They can be classified into: inherited conditions, autoimmune diseases, and acquired disorders (from injury or overuse).

Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 12 · Tue, Nov 3slide 5 of 32
Notebook p. 71 · Focused Notes: Muscle strain
Focused notes

Muscle strain

  • A strain is a tear in muscle fibers from overstretching or overuse. People call it a pulled muscle.
  • Symptoms: sudden sharp pain, swelling, and weakness.
  • Treatment: rest, ice, compression, elevation, then slow stretching and strengthening.

Notebook p. 71 · Focused Notes: Muscle strain
Acquired · injury

Muscle strain (pulled muscle)

  • Cause: overstretching or tearing of muscle fibers.
  • Symptoms: pain, swelling, reduced mobility, tenderness.
  • Treatment: R.I.C.E. — Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation.

Focused notes

Muscle cramp

  • A cramp is a sudden, painful contraction that you cannot control.
  • Common causes: tired muscles, dehydration, or low electrolytes like sodium and potassium.
  • Treatment: gently stretch, drink fluids, and rest the muscle.

Notebook p. 71 · Focused Notes: Muscle cramp
Acquired · involuntary

Muscle cramps

  • Cause: involuntary contractions from dehydration, fatigue, or electrolyte imbalance.
  • Symptoms: sharp pain, tightness, temporary loss of function.
  • Prevention: proper hydration, stretching, and warm-up.

Notebook p. 72 · Focused Notes: Muscular dystrophy
Focused notes

Muscular dystrophy

  • Muscular dystrophy is a genetic disorder. The most common type is missing a protein called dystrophin.
  • Without it, muscle fibers are damaged each time they contract and slowly break down.
  • Symptoms: weakness that gets worse over time. There is no cure yet, but therapy helps.

Notebook p. 72 · Focused Notes: Muscular dystrophy
Inherited

Muscular dystrophy (MD)

  • Cause: genetic mutations prevent production of dystrophin, a protein needed for muscle stability.
  • Symptoms: progressive muscle weakness, loss of mobility, respiratory and cardiac complications.
  • Connection: homeostasis is disrupted as muscles lose strength and coordination.

Notebook p. 72 · Focused Notes: Muscular dystrophy
A fourth condition

Atrophy

Muscle fibers shrink from disuse — not disease. Common after a limb is in a cast, or after very little muscle use, like an astronaut in low gravity.

Dystrophin and the nerve signal are both normal. Unlike the other disorders today, it is reversible with exercise.
