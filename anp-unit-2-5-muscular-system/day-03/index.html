Today's goal

Students will be able to identify the parts inside a skeletal muscle fiber, by matching each structure to its job, so that they can explain how these parts work together to enable contraction.

Focused notes

A muscle fiber is a muscle cell

  • A skeletal muscle cell is called a muscle fiber because it is long and thin like a thread.
  • Muscle words often start with sarco-, which means flesh.
  • The fiber's cell membrane is the sarcolemma. Its cytoplasm is the sarcoplasm.

The cell membrane

Sarcolemma

The cell membrane of the muscle fiber.

  • Controls what enters and exits the cell.
  • Conducts electrical impulses — like a fuse wire, carrying the signal to contract.

Notebook p. 15 · Focused Notes: Parts that carry the signal
Focused notes

Parts that carry the signal

  • The sarcolemma carries the electrical signal across the outside of the fiber, like wiring.
  • T-tubules are tunnels that carry the signal deep inside the fiber.
  • The sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) stores calcium. When the signal arrives, the SR releases calcium to start the contraction.

Notebook p. 15 · Focused Notes: Parts that carry the signal
Reaching inside

T-tubules

Extensions of the sarcolemma that penetrate into the cell.

They carry the signal deep into the muscle fiber, so the whole fiber contracts at once instead of just the outer edge.

Find it in the picture

The tubes that dip down from the sarcolemma and wrap around each myofibril, labeled T-tubule.

Notebook p. 16 · Focused Notes: Parts that do the work
Focused notes

Parts that do the work

  • Myofibrils are long bundles packed inside the fiber. They are the parts that actually shorten.
  • Mitochondria make ATP, the energy for contraction. Muscle fibers have lots of them.
  • Nuclei control making new proteins and repairing the fiber after exercise.

The contracting parts

Myofibrils

Long, cylindrical organelles that fill the muscle fiber.

They contain repeating sarcomeres — the parts that actually contract.

Find it in the picture

One myofibril is pulled out of the fiber (bottom). The repeating sections between Z discs are sarcomeres.

The power plants

Mitochondria

Numerous energy-producing organelles packed throughout the fiber.

They supply ATP for contraction.

The control centers

Nuclei

Many nuclei per fiber, sitting near the edge of the cell.

They regulate protein production along the whole length of the cell.

Find it in the pictures

Top: the label Nucleus (upper left) points to one of the oval nuclei on the surface of the fiber.
Bottom: real muscle under a microscope. Each long band is one fiber; the dark blue ovals are its many nuclei.

SarcolemmaMembrane surrounding the muscle fiberConducts impulses, like electrical wiring that carries signals across the muscle cell.
SarcoplasmCytoplasm of the muscle fiberHolds energy stores (glycogen, myoglobin), like a storage room filled with fuel for contractions.
