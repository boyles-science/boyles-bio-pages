Why this matters

Every move you make

Picking up a backpack. Blinking. Running to catch a bus. Every one of these depends on the same microscopic event happening billions of times: a sarcomere shortening.

Today you'll find out exactly what happens inside a muscle fiber to make that shortening happen — and what can go wrong.

Notebook p. 27 · Focused Notes: The sarcomere
Quick review

The sarcomere

  • A sarcomere is the unit that contracts. It runs from one Z-disc to the next.
  • Actin (thin filaments) is attached to the Z-discs.
  • Myosin (thick filaments) sits in the middle and has little heads that can grab actin.

Not a blank in your notes: thin filaments also carry troponin and tropomyosin, two proteins that cover the spots where myosin can attach. You will see them again on the Calcium slide.

Notebook p. 27 · Focused Notes: The sliding filament theory
Focused notes

The sliding filament theory

  • When a muscle contracts, myosin heads grab actin and pull it toward the center.
  • The actin slides past the myosin. The Z-discs move closer together.
  • The sarcomere gets shorter, but actin and myosin stay the same length.
Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 5 · Fri, Oct 23slide 10 of 39
Notebook p. 27 · Focused Notes: The sliding filament theory
The sliding filament theory

The filaments do not get shorter.

Key idea

The thin filaments slide past the thick filaments, pulling the ends of the sarcomere closer together. The filaments are the same length whether the muscle is relaxed or contracted. Only the overlap changes.

This sliding happens at many sarcomeres at once, all along a muscle fiber. That is why the whole muscle shortens.

Notebook p. 28 · Focused Notes: The four-step cycle
What starts the slide

Calcium

1
A nerve signal tells the muscle fiber to contract.
2
The fiber releases calcium from storage inside the cell.
3
Calcium binds to troponin. This moves tropomyosin out of the way.
4
Moving tropomyosin uncovers the spots on actin where a myosin head can attach.
Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 5 · Fri, Oct 23slide 14 of 39
Notebook p. 28 · Focused Notes: The four-step cycle
Focused notes

The four-step cycle

  • 1. Attach: a myosin head grabs actin (a cross-bridge).
  • 2. Power stroke: the head pivots and pulls actin in.
  • 3. Detach: ATP makes myosin let go.
  • 4. Reset: ATP re-cocks the head so it can grab again. Calcium must be present to uncover the spots on actin.
Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 5 · Fri, Oct 23slide 15 of 39
Notebook p. 28 · Focused Notes: The four-step cycle
The cross-bridge cycle · steps 1–2

Attach, then pull

1
Attach. A myosin head attaches to the uncovered spot on actin. This attached pair is called a cross-bridge.
2
Pull. The myosin head bends and pulls the thin filament toward the center of the sarcomere. This is the power stroke.
Unit 2.5 Muscular System · Day 5 · Fri, Oct 23slide 16 of 39
Notebook p. 28 · Focused Notes: The four-step cycle
The cross-bridge cycle · steps 3–4

Detach, then reset

3
Detach. ATP attaches to the myosin head. This makes the head let go of actin.
4
Reset. The myosin head uses the ATP's energy to stand back up, ready to attach again further along the filament.

Watch, then try
Partner activity

Act out the cycle

Set up

Face your partner. Stand a little more than an arm's length apart.

Partner A = actin

Hold one arm straight out, palm down. Keep it straight the whole time.

Partner B = myosin head

Cup one hand into a C shape. You do the four steps.

1
Attach. Hook your C hand around your partner's forearm, near the wrist.
2
Power stroke. Bend your elbow. Pull your hand toward your shoulder.
3
Detach. Say "ATP!" and open your hand to let go.
4
Reset. Reach out again. Grab a little farther up the arm, closer to the elbow.

Repeat 5 times, then switch roles. Watch: does Partner A's arm get shorter, or do you get closer together?

Level up · two hands

Partner B, use both hands as two myosin heads. Take turns: one hand pulls while the other resets. One hand always holds on, so the arm never slips back. Real muscle works this way.

Notebook p. 28 · Focused Notes: The four-step cycle
Why this repeats

One pull is not enough

  • One power stroke pulls the filament only a short distance — like one pull of a rope.
  • As long as calcium stays high and ATP is available, myosin heads attach, do a power stroke, detach, and reset again and again.
  • Thousands of these cycles happen at the same time, all along the sarcomere.
Remember

Without ATP, myosin heads cannot let go of actin. Without calcium, the binding spots stay covered and no cross-bridges can form.

Lab

Sliding Filament Model

Build a sarcomere from paper strips. Show relaxed and contracted, and measure the difference.

1
Cut out the pieces on notebook page 33: 2 Z-discs, 4 actin strips, and 1 myosin strip. Cut only on the dashed lines.
2
Tape 2 actin strips to each Z-disc, pointing inward. Lay the myosin strip in the middle, between the actin strips.
3
Relaxed: place the Z-discs about 12 cm apart, with little overlap. Measure Z-disc to Z-disc.
4
Contracted: slide the actin toward the center until the Z-discs are about 9 cm apart. Measure again.
5
Narrate the four steps of the cycle to your partner, naming calcium and ATP.
