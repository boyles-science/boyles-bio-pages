Terms
  • radiation: energy given off by unstable atoms. Some kinds can break DNA. It is a mutagen.
  • mutagen: something in the environment that damages DNA and causes mutations (for example, UV light).
  • body cell: any cell that is not a sperm or egg, or a cell that makes them. Mutations here are not inherited.
  • germline: the cells that make sperm or eggs, and the sperm and eggs themselves. Mutations here can reach a child.
  • new mutation: a mutation in a child that neither parent has in their body cells. Scientists also call it a de novo mutation.
  • thyroid: a gland in the neck. It uses iodine to make hormones.
  • iodine-131: a radioactive form of iodine. Half of it decays in about 8 days.

Card A · Thyroid cancer in children

Iodine-131 fell on grass. Cows ate the grass. Children drank the milk. The body moves iodine into the thyroid, so the radiation collected there.

What was measuredFinding
When cases appearedChildhood thyroid cancer cases began to rise 4 to 5 years after the accident.
How much the rate rose (Belarus and Ukraine, ages 14 and under)5 to 10 times higher in 1991–1994 than in 1986–1990.
Total cases by 2005More than 6,000, in people who were children or teens in 1986.

Sources: World Health Organization; UNSCEAR data in Japan Ministry of the Environment radiation booklet.

Card B · Children of exposed parents (Yeager and others, 2021, Science)

Scientists read the full DNA of children and both of their parents. They counted each child's new mutations.

Study detailFinding
Who was studied130 children from 105 mother-father pairs. In each family, at least one parent was a cleanup worker or was moved out of a contaminated area.
When the children were born1987 to 2002, from 46 weeks to 15 years after the accident.
Parents' radiation doseRanged from low to high across families.
Number of new mutationsNo increase. Counts were similar to children of unexposed parents in earlier studies, whatever the parents' dose.
Best predictor of new mutations (from many studies)The father's age. Each extra year adds about 0.64 to 1.51 new mutations.

Source: Yeager, M. and others (2021). Lack of transgenerational effects of ionizing radiation exposure from the Chernobyl accident. Science 372.

Card C · Barn swallows near Chernobyl (Ellegren and others, 1997, Nature)

Barn swallows are small birds that often nest in barns and other buildings. Scientists studied swallows breeding close to Chernobyl. They compared them with swallows from comparison areas elsewhere in Ukraine and in Italy.

What was measuredFinding
Partial albinism (some feathers white because they lack color)More common near Chernobyl. Family patterns showed it was at least partly passed from parents to chicks.
New mutations passed from parent to chick, in 2 fast-changing DNA regions2 to 10 times higher near Chernobyl than in the comparison areas.

Source: Ellegren, H. and others (1997). Fitness loss and germline mutations in barn swallows breeding in Chernobyl. Nature 389, 593–596.
