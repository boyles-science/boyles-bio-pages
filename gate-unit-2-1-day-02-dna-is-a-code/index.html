Today's goal

I can name the four bases in DNA and use the pairing rule. I can explain that the order of the bases, not only which bases are there, carries information.

Terms

Base: one of the four building blocks of DNA. Their letters are A, T, C, and G.
Strand: a row of bases. DNA has two strands that pair up.
Gene: a section of DNA. Many genes hold the instructions for making a protein.
Order: the sequence in which the bases appear.

Think about letters. The letters E, A, and T can spell EAT, TEA, or ATE. The same letters in a different order make a different word.

Part 1: The pairing rule

The two strands of DNA are held together by pairs of bases. A pairs with T. C pairs with G.

Going deeper: how many orders? (optional)
